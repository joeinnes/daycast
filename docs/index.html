World News

Europe Sleepwalks into Another Energy Crisis

Source: BBC News

The Bank of England held interest rates at three point seven five per cent today, but the real story is the tone shift. Governor Andrew Bailey warned the Bank is ready to raise rates if the energy price shock from the Iran conflict persists, reversing months of cautious cutting. Brent crude sits above a hundred and eight dollars a barrel after Israeli strikes on Iran's South Pars gasfield and the near-total shutdown of tanker traffic through the Strait of Hormuz. The disruption is not just a British problem. Across Europe, governments are scrambling for alternatives as the continent confronts its second major energy crisis in four years. Beijing's oil reserves and renewable energy push are being tested too, but Europe, having only recently weaned itself off Russian gas dependence, is especially exposed.

Hungary Deepens Ukraine Rift as Election Nears

Source: Telex

Viktor Orbán continues to block a ninety-billion euro European Union loan to Ukraine, linking it to a dispute over Druzhba pipeline oil deliveries. Twenty of twenty-six other member state leaders publicly criticised the move this week, and the European Council president repeated that the reversal breaches EU legal principles. Budapest has also banned three Ukrainian citizens from entering Hungary and the Schengen area — a retired intelligence officer, a former presidential adviser, and a military figure — as the bilateral relationship deteriorates further. In a striking development at the government press conference, Minister Gergely Gulyás said that if Donald Trump requested military assistance in freeing the Strait of Hormuz, Hungary would consider it. With the parliamentary election on the twelfth of April approaching, each of these moves carries domestic as well as diplomatic weight.

Hungarian Society Has Slipped Dramatically, ELTE Researchers Find

Source: Telex

Researchers at ELTE university published findings showing what they describe as a dramatic decline in Hungarian social stratification over the past four years. The proportion of people in the lowest income segments has grown substantially while the upper-middle class has visibly thinned. The researchers also noted a high share of respondents who conceal their political preferences, making election polling unusually uncertain ahead of April's vote.

UK News

Government Backtracks on AI Copyright After Artist Outcry

Source: BBC News

The UK government has dropped its preferred approach to artificial intelligence and copyright, which would have allowed tech firms to train models on copyrighted works unless rights holders explicitly opted out. Technology Secretary Liz Kendall confirmed the reversal after a consultation drew more than eleven thousand five hundred responses, with the overwhelming majority from creators and publishers rejecting the proposal. Artists including Sir Elton John and Dua Lipa had campaigned against it. The government now says it has no preferred option, with four proposals still on the table ranging from leaving copyright law unchanged to a full waiver granting AI companies unrestricted access. UK Music's chief executive called it a major victory for campaigners, but the lack of a clear path forward means uncertainty continues for both sides.

A Sufficiently Detailed Spec Is Code

Source: Hacker News

Gabriella Gonzalez has published an essay arguing that the premise behind agentic coding — write a detailed specification, let the model generate the implementation — rests on two false assumptions. The first is that specifications are simpler than code. The second is that writing them requires more thoughtfulness than coding. She demonstrates through OpenAI's own Symphony project that supposedly spec-driven output actually contains prose database schemas, pseudocode, and explicit code sections. The piece quotes Dijkstra on the irreducible complexity of interfaces. It is a sharp counterpoint to the "just describe what you want" school of AI-assisted development, and it has gathered over five hundred points on Hacker News.

Quantum Cryptography Pioneers Win the Turing Award

Source: BBC News

Charles Bennett and Gilles Brassard have received the twenty twenty-five ACM Turing Award for their foundational work in quantum information science. Their BB84 protocol, introduced in nineteen eighty-four, demonstrated that two parties could establish a secret encryption key with security guaranteed by the laws of physics, not by computational assumptions. Any attempt at eavesdropping leaves detectable traces before information is compromised. It is the first time the award has recognised work rooted in quantum physics, and it matters because BB84's security model holds even against future quantum computers — a property no classical encryption scheme can claim.

Adrian Newey Departs Aston Martin as Wheatley Takes the Reins

Source: Telex

Adrian Newey, the most decorated designer in Formula One history, has resigned from his role at Aston Martin after less than a year. Jonathan Wheatley, previously at Audi, replaces him.
