World News

The US lifts sanctions on its own enemy's oil as prices hit new highs

Follow-up

Source: BBC News

The Trump administration has taken the remarkable step of temporarily lifting sanctions on roughly a hundred and forty million barrels of Iranian oil already at sea, issuing a thirty-day waiver that allows buyers to purchase Iranian crude loaded before Friday. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the goal is to bring prices down after Brent crude settled at a hundred and twelve dollars a barrel, its highest since the war began. The move amounts to an admission that three weeks into the conflict, Washington is running out of options to contain the energy shock it helped trigger.

Iranian man and companion arrested at Britain's nuclear submarine base

Source: BBC News

An Iranian citizen and a woman were arrested on Thursday after attempting to enter the Faslane naval base in western Scotland, home to Britain's nuclear submarine fleet. The pair did not force their way in but asked at the gate without the required identification, and were detained shortly afterwards for suspicious behaviour. The Ministry of Defence and police are investigating. In the current climate, even a clumsy attempt at access to a nuclear facility draws immediate attention.

Trump compares the attack on Iran to Pearl Harbor in meeting with Japan's prime minister

Source: BBC News

During a meeting with Japan's prime minister, Donald Trump drew an explicit comparison between the American-led strikes on Iran and the nineteen forty-one Japanese attack on Pearl Harbor. The remark was reportedly intended to convey the scale and surprise of the operation. The diplomatic fallout from the analogy is still being measured, but it arrives at a moment when the administration's messaging on the war has been anything but consistent.

Israel strikes Syria, saying it will not tolerate attacks on the Druze

Source: BBC News

Israel launched strikes against Syrian army camps and a command centre in southern Syria overnight, responding to clashes between government-aligned forces and Druze factions in the Sweida area. The Israeli defence minister said Israel would not allow the Syrian regime to exploit the Iran war to harm the Druze population. Damascus condemned the strikes as an assault on sovereignty. It is another front opening at a moment when the region can least afford one.

A jury finds Elon Musk misled Twitter investors ahead of the forty-four billion dollar takeover

Source: BBC News

A San Francisco jury has found that Elon Musk made materially false and misleading statements to Twitter investors during the chaotic lead-up to his twenty twenty-two acquisition of the platform. The jury identified two specific tweets as misleading, though it stopped short of finding a deliberate scheme to defraud. Damages could reach up to two point six billion dollars. The verdict is a significant legal setback even if the broader fraud claim did not stick.

National Election Commission fines public media for biased coverage

Source: Telex

Hungary's National Election Commission has fined the state public broadcaster millions of forints for biased election coverage. The commission also filed a complaint about social media posts linked to the ruling party. With the April election approaching, it is a rare instance of the country's own electoral body formally calling out what many have long argued is a captured media landscape.

Fertiliser supply disrupted as Hungary lacks emergency reserves

Source: Telex

Global fertiliser shortages linked to the Iran conflict have reached Hungary at the worst possible moment, right as spring planting begins. Hungary has no emergency fertiliser reserves to draw on, and suppliers warn that food prices could rise significantly within months. Taken alongside the fuel rationing at petrol stations reported earlier this week, the war's effects on everyday Hungarian life are becoming harder to ignore.

Space

NASA's Artemis Two moon rocket rolls to the launch pad for a possible April liftoff

Source: BBC News

NASA's Space Launch System rocket began its slow journey to Launch Pad 39B at Kennedy Space Centre early on Friday, carrying the Orion spacecraft that will send four astronauts around the Moon and back. If all goes well, the mission could launch as early as the first of April, making it the first crewed flight beyond low Earth orbit in more than fifty years. The crew includes NASA astronauts Reid Wiseman, Victor Glover, and Christina Koch, alongside Canadian astronaut Jeremy Hansen. After years of delays and a hydrogen leak scare earlier this year, this rollout feels like a genuine turning point.

ArXiv, the pioneering preprint server, declares independence from Cornell

Source: Hacker News

ArXiv, the preprint server that transformed how scientists share research when it launched in nineteen ninety-one, is leaving Cornell University after more than two decades. From the first of July it becomes an independent nonprofit, with a search underway for its first chief executive. The move is about money and scale. ArXiv expects to host more than three hundred thousand preprints this year, and an academic institution was never designed to run global research infrastructure indefinitely. Cornell and the Simons Foundation have ensured the new entity starts with enough funding to operate for several years.

A team rewrote their Rust WebAssembly parser in TypeScript and it got faster

Source: Hacker News

The team behind OpenUI published a post explaining how they replaced a Rust-compiled WebAssembly parser with a native TypeScript implementation and saw performance improve. The result cuts against the assumption that compiled languages always win in the browser. The discussion on Hacker News, with nearly ninety comments, digs into the specifics of when the WASM boundary overhead outweighs the raw speed advantage of Rust.

Someone built a Bluesky client in Fortran, because of course they did

Source: Hacker News

A Show HN post this week introduced Fortransky, a terminal-only client for Bluesky and the AT Protocol, written entirely in Fortran. Yes, that Fortran. It is a fun novelty, but it also says something about the AT Protocol's openness that a language from the nineteen fifties can speak to it. The project is on GitHub and works, which is more than enough to earn a mention.
