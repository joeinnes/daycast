A federal judge blocks the Pentagon's attempt to blacklist Anthropic

Source: BBC News / Hacker News

A federal judge in California has issued a preliminary injunction blocking the Pentagon from enforcing its designation of Anthropic as a supply chain risk. Judge Rita Lin wrote that the government was attempting to cripple the company and chill public debate, calling the action classic First Amendment retaliation. The dispute centres on Anthropic's refusal to accept new contract terms that it feared would open the door to mass surveillance and fully autonomous weapons. For now, Claude and Anthropic's other tools will continue to be used across government.

Nepal's former prime minister arrested over a deadly protest crackdown

Source: BBC News

Nepal's former prime minister K.P. Sharma Oli has been arrested in connection with a crackdown on protests last year that killed more than seventy people. The uprising ultimately toppled his government, and the country recently swore in a new prime minister, Balendra Shah, a former rapper who won office on an anti-corruption platform. The arrest signals that accountability for the crackdown is being pursued under the new administration.

UK

A jury finds Meta and YouTube liable in a landmark social media addiction trial

Follow-up

Source: BBC News

A California jury has found Meta and YouTube liable on all counts in what is being called a landmark case on social media addiction. The plaintiff, a twenty-year-old woman who began using YouTube at six and Instagram at nine, was awarded six million dollars in combined compensatory and punitive damages. The jury concluded that both platforms were deliberately designed to be addictive and that executives knew this. Hundreds of similar cases are now in the pipeline, and the ruling could force significant changes to how these platforms handle younger users.
