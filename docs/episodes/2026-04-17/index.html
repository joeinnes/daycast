Hungary

Nagy Márton retires from politics

Source: Telex.hu

Five days after Fidesz's landslide defeat, outgoing economy minister Nagy Márton has announced he is quitting politics altogether. Nagy was one of the most prominent architects of the last government's economic policy, and his exit is another sign that senior Fidesz figures are reading the writing on the wall rather than regrouping for opposition. Telex notes the detail, much mocked during the campaign, that Nagy had been staying in Brussels hotels at seven to eight hundred thousand forints a night — a symbol for many voters of how out of touch the outgoing government had become.

Tisza wants Hungary to adopt the euro by twenty thirty

Source: Telex.hu

Péter Magyar's incoming Tisza government has set itself a striking new economic target: to create the conditions for Hungary to adopt the euro by twenty thirty. Tisza's fiscal policy expert told Telex that under favourable circumstances, it could happen even sooner. Euro adoption was a firm political red line under Fidesz, so this is one of the clearest signals yet that economic policy will look fundamentally different under Magyar, and a direct bet that tighter integration with the European Union will unlock growth the old model could not.

Removing President Sulyok may be harder than the supermajority suggests

Follow-up

Source: Telex.hu

Péter Magyar has called on President Tamás Sulyok to step aside, but Telex has walked through what happens if he refuses. Any impeachment route passes through the Constitutional Court, whose members Parliament cannot remove. There is a more convoluted legal workaround, but constitutional lawyers say it would look very bad for a government that has just campaigned on restoring the rule of law. In other words, the two-thirds supermajority is powerful, but it does not simply delete the old guard.

András Schiff will play in Hungary again

Source: Telex.hu

The world-renowned pianist András Schiff has announced he will perform in Hungary again, after spending much of the Orbán era refusing to play his home country. Schiff said that for the first time in sixteen years, he is proud of Hungary, and that he plans to play before as large an audience as possible. His return is a small but resonant cultural marker of the change now under way.

World News

Israel and Lebanon begin a ten-day ceasefire

Follow-up

Source: BBC News

A ten-day ceasefire between Israel and Lebanon is now in effect, with Iran-backed Hezbollah voicing support, while United States–Iran negotiations continue in parallel. The BBC's Lucy Williamson reports that few Israelis see the truce as a durable way out of the conflict with Hezbollah, and the pause took the Israeli public largely by surprise. Still, it is the first real break in months of escalation along the border, and the diplomatic window it opens is what matters most over the next ten days.

Russia launches its deadliest aerial attack on Ukraine in months

Source: BBC News

Russia has carried out its deadliest aerial assault on Ukraine in several months, killing at least eighteen people in overnight strikes across multiple cities. Moscow says a Ukrainian drone attack in reply killed two people inside Russia. Both exchanges came after a short-lived Orthodox Easter truce, and the scale of the Russian barrage is a blunt signal that even as the Middle East edges towards calm, the war in Ukraine is intensifying rather than fading into the background.

A Russian drone briefly enters Romanian airspace

Source: Telex.hu

Romanian air defence radars detected two Russian drones near the border overnight, one of which briefly entered Romanian airspace before turning back. No damage or injuries have been reported, but the incursion is the latest in a growing pattern of spillover from the war in Ukraine into neighbouring European Union and NATO territory. For Hungary's neighbours in particular, the edges of the conflict are becoming uncomfortably close.

Andy Kershaw, Radio One DJ and Live Aid presenter, dies at sixty-six

Source: BBC News

Andy Kershaw, the former BBC Radio One DJ who helped champion world music on British airwaves and presented the Live Aid concert in nineteen eighty-five, has died at the age of sixty-six. Kershaw's eclectic taste, his reporting from war zones, and his relentless advocacy for music from outside the Anglo-American mainstream made him one of the most distinctive voices in Radio One's history. His death marks the end of an era for a generation of listeners who discovered entire continents of music through his shows.

Anthropic releases Claude Opus four point seven

Source: Hacker News

Anthropic has released Claude Opus four point seven, the latest generation of its flagship large language model. The Hacker News thread, with more than eighteen hundred points, is heavily focused on how the new model performs on coding tasks and long-horizon agentic workflows, particularly against Claude Sonnet and a new crop of open-weights competitors from Chinese labs. For anyone building with Claude, the headline is that the performance jump on genuinely long tasks is the most substantial upgrade so far this year.

Source: Hacker News

Alibaba has released Qwen three point six thirty-five B A three B, an open-weights coding model using a mixture-of-experts design, under what the team calls agentic coding capabilities now open to everyone. With over eleven hundred points on Hacker News, the discussion centres on how a relatively small model can hold its own against closed frontier systems, and what it means for the open ecosystem that the best open weights are now routinely arriving from Chinese labs rather than American ones.

Finance ministers raise serious alarms over the Mythos artificial intelligence model

Source: BBC News

Finance ministers and senior bankers are raising serious concerns about a new artificial intelligence model called Mythos, which experts say has an unprecedented ability to identify and exploit cyber-security weaknesses. The worry is that Mythos dramatically lowers the bar for sophisticated attacks on banking and financial infrastructure. This is a rare moment of high-level financial policy attention directed at a specific model, and a sign that the cyber-security implications of frontier artificial intelligence are becoming concrete rather than theoretical.
