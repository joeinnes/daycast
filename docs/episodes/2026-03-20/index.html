World News

Iran Conflict Deepens as Israel Strikes Tehran Again

Follow-up

Source: BBC News / Telex

The Middle East situation is escalating fast. Israel has carried out another attack on Tehran, with Benjamin Netanyahu confirming Israel acted alone in the earlier strike on an Iranian gas field and indicating that Donald Trump requested no further such operations. A drone strike hit a Kuwaiti refinery on Friday morning, raising the prospect of disruption spreading beyond Iran itself. Nearly a hundred ships have passed through the Strait of Hormuz since the crisis began, and the question of who is getting through, and who is not, is becoming a geopolitical story in its own right. Trump is reportedly furious with European allies over what he sees as insufficient support, and some European leaders are quietly debating whether to push back or comply. The knock-on effects are already tangible: India faces gas shortages that may push the country back towards dirtier fuels, and energy markets are in turmoil.

UK News

Bank of England Holds Rates as Iran War Sends Energy Shock Through Economy

Follow-up

Source: BBC News

The Bank of England voted unanimously to hold interest rates at three point seven five per cent, citing the energy price shock from the Iran conflict as the key factor. Policymakers had been expected to continue cutting borrowing costs, but the war has reversed that trajectory entirely. The BBC's economics editor Faisal Islam notes the effects will reverberate through the UK economy for months and extend well beyond interest rates alone.

Fuel Crisis Hits Hungarian Gas Stations

Source: Telex

Hungary's fuel situation is deteriorating. Increasing numbers of small petrol stations are restricting how much fuel customers can buy at one time, with some imposing two-litre limits. Operators say most have not received state fuel reserves that would allow them to sell at price-capped rates profitably. Some station owners are now planning pre-election protests, calling the situation worse than the energy crisis of twenty twenty-two.

Noq: A New QUIC Implementation in Rust

Source: Hacker News

The team behind Iroh, a peer-to-peer networking library, has announced Noq, a fresh QUIC protocol implementation written in Rust. QUIC underpins HTTP/3 and is increasingly important for low-latency networking. The project joins a growing ecosystem of Rust networking primitives and is designed to be modular enough for use outside the Iroh stack.
