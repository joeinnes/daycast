## Tech & Developer

### Bram Cohen outlines Mañana, a new approach to version control
source: Hacker News
hn_url: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47478401

Bram Cohen, the inventor of BitTorrent, has published a detailed proposal for a new version control system called Mañana. The core argument is that Git's model, despite its dominance, makes several fundamental trade-offs that limit what version control can be, particularly around merge handling and history representation. Mañana proposes a different data model that Cohen says would make branching, merging, and cherry-picking significantly more predictable. With nearly five hundred points and two hundred and seventy comments on Hacker News, it is clearly striking a nerve among developers who have felt the pain points Cohen describes. Whether anyone can actually dethrone Git remains to be seen, but the technical argument is serious.

### Reports of code's death are greatly exaggerated
source: Hacker News
hn_url: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47476315

Steve Krouse has published a thoughtful counterpoint to the narrative that AI coding tools will eliminate the need to write code. His argument centres on what he calls precision: the idea that the gap between describing what you want in natural language and specifying it precisely enough for a computer to execute remains vast for any non-trivial system. The essay has drawn over three hundred points and two hundred and fifty comments, with the discussion splitting between those who think the current trajectory will close that gap and those who see it as fundamental. For anyone whose livelihood involves writing code, it is a debate worth following.

### Flash-MoE demonstrates running a three hundred and ninety-seven billion parameter model on a laptop
source: Hacker News
hn_url: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47476422

A new open-source project called Flash-MoE has shown that a mixture-of-experts model with three hundred and ninety-seven billion parameters can run on consumer hardware, specifically a laptop. The trick is in how the model activates only a fraction of its parameters for any given inference, keeping memory and compute requirements within reach of machines that would otherwise have no business running models of that size. The project has gathered over three hundred points on Hacker News and represents another step in the ongoing democratisation of large language models.

### Quantum encryption pioneers Charles Bennett and Gilles Brassard win Turing Award
source: BBC News / Hacker News
hn_url: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47476566

Charles Bennett and Gilles Brassard have been awarded the Turing Award, computing's highest honour, for their pioneering work on quantum cryptography. In nineteen eighty-four, the pair developed the BB84 protocol, the first practical method for using the laws of quantum mechanics to secure communications. Their work underpins the field of quantum key distribution, which promises encryption that is mathematically unbreakable regardless of an adversary's computational power. As conventional encryption faces the long-term threat of quantum computers, the irony is that quantum mechanics also provides the defence.
