Hungary

Péter Magyar's Tisza Party wins a two-thirds supermajority, ending sixteen years of Orbán rule

Then & Now
First covered 2026-03-25 when record overseas voter registrations signalled high turnout

Source: BBC News / Telex

Hungary held parliamentary elections on Sunday and the result was decisive. Péter Magyar's Tisza Party has won a two-thirds constitutional supermajority, ending Viktor Orbán's sixteen-year grip on power. Magyar, a forty-five-year-old former Fidesz insider turned opposition leader, described the result as the end of a two-decade nightmare and the beginning of Hungary's awakening. European leaders have welcomed the outcome, while Moscow has been notably silent. Orbán has called a Fidesz assembly for the twenty-eighth of April but has not yet conceded publicly.

What happens next before the new government takes office

Source: Telex

The constitutional clock is now ticking. President Tamás Sulyok must convene the inaugural session of the new parliament by the twelfth of May, at which point he will formally propose a candidate for prime minister. Until then, the outgoing Fidesz government retains full authority and the current parliament can still pass legislation, though a Tisza supermajority can subsequently repeal anything passed in the interim. Magyar is meeting Sulyok on Wednesday.

Markets surge on the Tisza victory as the forint hits record strength against the euro

Source: Telex

Financial markets have responded with enthusiasm. The forint has strengthened so sharply that Hungarian average wages now exceed those of three other European Union member states in euro terms. Bond and equity prices have also risen. Analysts say the rally reflects expectations that frozen EU funds will now be unlocked, but caution that the incoming government faces serious fiscal challenges, including a large first-quarter budget deficit. Fitch Ratings says the change of government improves the outlook for EU funding, though it warns the economic headwinds are real.
