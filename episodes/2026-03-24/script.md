source: BBC News

An Air Canada flight arriving from Montreal struck a fire truck on the runway after landing; both crew members of the fire vehicle were killed in the impact. Passenger seat belts are being credited with preventing more serious casualties among those on board. The airport's air traffic controller has admitted an error, and an investigation is under way. ### Slovenia becomes the first European Union member state to ration fuel purchases
source: BBC News

Slovenia has introduced a limit of fifty litres per fuel purchase, making it the first country in the European Union to formally ration at the pump since the Iran conflict began driving global energy prices higher. The measure is designed to prevent panic buying and ensure equitable distribution as diesel and petrol supplies tighten across the region. For Hungary and its neighbours, Slovenia's move is a warning signal: the energy shock from the Middle East is no longer an abstraction but is now reshaping daily life within the bloc. The European Commission has so far stopped short of coordinating a union-wide response, and the pressure on member states to act unilaterally is growing.

### Italy's Meloni suffers defeat in constitutional referendum
source: BBC News

Italian voters have rejected a constitutional reform backed by Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni, handing her government a significant political defeat. The result is being read less as a verdict on the specific reform — which concerned the direct election of the head of government — and more as a referendum on Meloni's four years in office. Turnout was relatively low, but the margin of defeat is wide enough that her allies cannot credibly dismiss it as a technical outcome. Meloni remains in government and retains a parliamentary majority, but her personal authority has taken a visible knock at a delicate moment for the Italian economy.

## Tech & Developer

### The United States bans consumer internet routers made outside America
source: BBC News / FCC
hn_url: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47495344

The Federal Communications Commission has updated its covered list to include nearly all major brands of consumer internet router manufactured outside the United States, effectively banning their import and sale on national security grounds. There are almost no major router brands that are manufactured in America, which means the practical consequence of the ruling is still being worked out: it is not clear whether existing routers will need to be replaced or whether the ban applies only to new purchases going forward. The ruling has drawn over two hundred and seventy points of discussion on Hacker News, with developers concerned both about the supply chain disruption and the surveillance and security implications of forcing consumers towards a narrower set of approved hardware. The decision sits at the intersection of trade policy, infrastructure security, and internet access, and the detail will matter enormously.

### A developer argues Rust's coherence rules are holding the ecosystem back
source: Hacker News
hn_url: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47490648

A blog post titled "An Incoherent Rust" has attracted over a hundred and sixty points on Hacker News by making the case that Rust's orphan rules — the constraints that prevent multiple implementations of a trait for the same type — are creating an ecosystem problem. The author's argument is that foundational crates like Serde become permanent fixtures precisely because the coherence rules prevent alternative serialisation libraries from gaining ecosystem traction, even when better options exist. The proposed solution is to treat trait implementations as named values that can be passed explicitly rather than resolved by the compiler, which would allow multiple implementations to coexist without breaking the type system's soundness guarantees. It is the kind of language evolution discussion that tends to run for years before anything changes, but the underlying tension is real and the post frames it clearly.

### Bombadil brings property-based testing to web user interfaces
source: Hacker News
hn_url: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47436592

Antithesis has released Bombadil, an open-source framework for property-based testing of web user interfaces. Where traditional front-end testing involves writing specific scenarios — click this button, check that element — property-based testing asks you to describe invariants: things that should always be true regardless of what sequence of actions a user takes. Bombadil then autonomously explores your application looking for sequences that violate those invariants. The project integrates with local development environments and continuous integration pipelines, and represents an interesting alternative to the brittle end-to-end test suites that most web teams end up maintaining. For anyone whose testing philosophy leans towards specification over scenario, it is worth a look.
