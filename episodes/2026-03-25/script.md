# Daily Briefing — Wednesday, 25 March 2026

It is Wednesday the twenty-fifth of March. The Iran war enters its twenty-sixth day with a tentative shift in tone, Hungary's election campaign is in full swing with record numbers registering to vote abroad, and the open-source world has a couple of stories worth paying attention to.

## World News

### Iran says it will allow non-hostile ships through the Strait of Hormuz, with conditions
source: Telex / BBC News
previously_covered: true
update_note: Iran offers conditional reopening of Hormuz shipping lane; previous coverage focused on Israel's expanded Lebanon operations and the energy crisis

Iran has announced it will permit non-hostile commercial vessels to pass through the Strait of Hormuz, attaching conditions that have not yet been fully spelled out. The move comes as oil supplies continue to tighten and BlackRock's Larry Fink warned that sustained prices at a hundred and fifty dollars a barrel would trigger a global recession. Meanwhile, Trump surprised observers by claiming that "very good and productive" talks have been held to end the conflict, though Iran has shown no public willingness to negotiate so far.

### Israel says it will take control of a large buffer zone in southern Lebanon
source: BBC News
previously_covered: true
update_note: Israel now declares it will hold the southern Lebanon buffer zone indefinitely; previous coverage reported the initial expansion of operations against Hezbollah

Israel's leadership has said it intends to take control of a substantial buffer zone in southern Lebanon and that thousands of displaced Lebanese residents will not be allowed to return until security in northern Israel improves. This marks a shift from what was initially framed as expanded military operations into what now looks like a longer-term territorial hold.

### Russia launches nine hundred and forty-eight drones at Ukraine in its largest attack over a single day
source: BBC News

Russia has carried out its largest drone attack on Ukraine to date, launching nine hundred and forty-eight drones over a twenty-four-hour period. Multiple cities were hit, with local officials reporting at least eight deaths and dozens of injuries. The sheer scale of the assault suggests a deliberate attempt to overwhelm Ukrainian air defences.

### Denmark's Social Democrats win the most votes but fall short of a majority
source: BBC News

Denmark went to the polls on Tuesday and Mette Frederiksen's Social Democrats won the most votes, but fell short of a parliamentary majority. It is the party's weakest result in over a century, despite a popularity surge earlier this year when Frederiksen confronted the United States over Greenland. Tough coalition negotiations lie ahead.

### Slovenia becomes the first EU country to introduce fuel rationing
source: BBC News

Slovenia has become the first European Union member state to introduce fuel rationing, capping purchases at fifty litres per driver per day. The measure is a direct consequence of the energy price shock from the Iran conflict. For Hungary next door, this is a signal of what could come if the crisis deepens further.

## UK

### Social media bans and digital curfews to be trialled on UK teenagers
source: BBC News

The UK government is launching a trial in which teenagers will face social media bans and digital curfews, with researchers interviewing the young people and their parents before and after to measure the impact. It follows the Netherlands' experience with phone bans in schools and a broader European push to limit children's screen time. The government will also interview participants to assess whether the restrictions actually change behaviour.

### The first female Archbishop of Canterbury is enthroned today
source: BBC News

The first woman to serve as Archbishop of Canterbury will be enthroned at Canterbury Cathedral today, with the Prince and Princess of Wales among the congregation. The ceremony reflects a significant milestone for the Church of England, and the new archbishop's background in nursing and care work is expected to shape her tenure.

## Hungary & Central Europe

### Record numbers register to vote at foreign missions ahead of Hungary's April election
source: Telex

A record sixty-five thousand six hundred and ninety-five Hungarians have registered to vote at diplomatic missions abroad, the highest number ever recorded. With the election now weeks away, polling from Research Centres shows ninety-eight per cent of Tisza Party supporters say they will definitely turn out. The combination of high overseas registration and strong opposition mobilisation is creating a very different electoral picture from recent years.

### AI disinformation campaigns target Hungary's opposition from both sides
source: Telex

Two separate disinformation campaigns have been identified in the run-up to Hungary's election. NewsGuard has found a thirty-four-account network on TikTok using artificial intelligence to support Orbán and attack the opposition. Separately, an AI-generated video circulating online falsely shows a Tisza Party candidate responding in Russian, in an apparent attempt to associate the opposition with Moscow. The Washington Post has reported on the latter.

### Slovakia's Fico warns that a Tisza victory could damage Slovak-Hungarian relations
source: Telex

Slovak prime minister Robert Fico has warned that a Tisza Party victory in Hungary's election could harm relations between the two countries. The intervention is notable because it positions Fico as openly backing Orbán's Fidesz in a neighbouring country's election, and frames the democratic opposition as a regional threat.

## Tech & Developer

### OpenAI shuts down Sora and ends its Disney partnership
source: BBC News / Hacker News
hn_url: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47508246

OpenAI is closing Sora, its AI video generation tool, less than two years after its launch sent shockwaves through the media industry. The shutdown also ends a partnership with Disney. The Hacker News discussion is running at over six hundred points, with many noting how quickly the hype cycle turned. The practical reality is that video generation at the quality level Sora promised turned out to be far harder to monetise than expected.

### Wine 11 rewrites how Linux runs Windows software, with major speed gains
source: Hacker News
hn_url: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47507150

Wine version eleven has landed with a fundamental rewrite of how Linux runs Windows applications, making changes at the kernel interaction level that deliver what the developers describe as massive speed improvements. The project, which has been running for over thirty years, continues to be one of the more remarkable pieces of open-source infrastructure. The Hacker News thread is over eight hundred points and full of benchmarks.

### Video.js is back under its original creator, rewritten to be eighty-eight per cent smaller
source: Hacker News
hn_url: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47506713

The creator of Video.js has retaken control of the project after sixteen years and released a complete rewrite that cuts the library's size by eighty-eight per cent. The new version ten beta strips out legacy compatibility in favour of modern browser APIs. For anyone building video players on the web, this is worth a look.

### Litellm packages on PyPI found to be compromised
source: Hacker News
hn_url: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47501426

Versions 1.82.7 and 1.82.8 of litellm, a popular Python library for working with large language model APIs, have been confirmed as compromised on PyPI. The issue was flagged as a Tell HN post and the associated GitHub issue details the supply chain attack. If you have either of those versions installed, update immediately.

### Arm announces an AGI-branded CPU architecture
source: Hacker News
hn_url: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47506251

Arm has announced what it is calling the Arm AGI CPU, a new processor architecture aimed at artificial intelligence workloads. The naming choice raised some eyebrows on Hacker News, since AGI in this context stands for something quite different from the usual meaning. The technical substance is a chip design optimised for inference at the edge.
