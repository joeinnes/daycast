It is Thursday the twenty-sixth of March, and today's briefing covers the latest on the Iran conflict as peace talk claims collide with explicit denials, stray Ukrainian drones landing in NATO territory, and the European Parliament facing a fresh push to reverse its rejection of mass message scanning.

## World News

### Iran rejects the idea of negotiations even as indirect channels remain open

The gulf between Washington and Tehran's public positions on ending the war is widening. ### Stray Ukrainian drones land in Estonia and Latvia
source: BBC News

Estonia and Latvia have both reported that Ukrainian drones landed on their territory during a massive Ukrainian attack on Russia's Baltic port of Ust-Luga. The incidents are the first time stray munitions from the war in Ukraine have struck NATO member states' soil. Neither country has suggested any hostile intent from Kyiv, but the events highlight the physical proximity of the conflict to the alliance's eastern flank.

### Meta and YouTube found liable in landmark social media addiction case
source: BBC News

A jury in the United States has found Meta and YouTube liable in what is being described as a landmark social media addiction trial, awarding six million dollars in damages. A separate court in New Mexico has ordered Meta to pay three hundred and seventy-five million dollars for misleading users over child safety on Instagram. The verdicts could set precedents for hundreds of similar cases, and will be watched closely in the UK where Parliament is actively debating social media restrictions for under-sixteens.

## UK

### British forces preparing to board Russian shadow fleet ships
source: BBC News

The UK is preparing for its military to board ships belonging to Russia's so-called shadow fleet operating in British waters. Prime Minister Keir Starmer made the announcement during a military summit in Finland, framing the move as part of efforts to starve Putin's war machine. The shadow fleet, a network of ageing tankers used to circumvent Western sanctions on Russian oil, has been a growing concern for coastal nations around the North Sea and Baltic.

### Former Google executive confirmed as new BBC director general
source: BBC News

Matt Brittin, formerly of Google, has been confirmed as the BBC's new director general. He described taking the role at what he called a moment of real risk yet also real opportunity for the corporation. The appointment of a tech executive to lead Britain's public broadcaster signals a continued shift in how the BBC sees its future, less as a traditional broadcaster and more as a digital platform competing for attention.

### Doctors announce six-day strike in England
source: BBC News

Doctors in England have announced a six-day strike beginning on the seventh of April after pay negotiations collapsed. ## Tech & Developer

### The European Parliament faces a new vote on mass scanning of private messages
source: Hacker News
hn_url: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47522709

The European People's Party is attempting to force a new vote today in the European Parliament on the so-called chat control proposal, which would require platforms to scan private messages and photos. The Parliament already voted against the measure, but the EPP is seeking to reverse that decision. With nearly nine hundred points on Hacker News, the developer community's opposition is fierce. The outcome of today's vote could reshape the encryption and privacy landscape across the European Union.

### GitHub changes its Copilot data usage policy
source: Hacker News
hn_url: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47521799

Starting on the twenty-fourth of April, GitHub will use interaction data from Copilot Free, Pro, and Pro Plus users to train its AI models unless users explicitly opt out. The data includes accepted suggestions, code context, file names, and feedback ratings. Business and Enterprise users are unaffected. GitHub says incorporating real-world developer data has already improved acceptance rates, but the default being opt-in rather than opt-out has drawn sharp criticism from developers who see it as a significant shift in the company's stance on user data.

### ARC-AGI-3 launches as the first interactive reasoning benchmark for AI
source: Hacker News
hn_url: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47521150

The ARC Prize team has released ARC-AGI-3, which it describes as the first interactive reasoning benchmark designed to measure human-like intelligence in AI agents. Unlike static benchmarks, it challenges agents to explore novel environments, learn goals dynamically, and adapt strategies based on experience. The philosophy is straightforward: as long as there is a gap between AI and human learning efficiency, we do not have artificial general intelligence. Every task is fully solvable by humans, making the comparison direct and the bar unambiguous.

### Prompt injection attacks remain an unsolved problem
source: Hacker News
hn_url: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47524519

Cal Paterson has published a clear-eyed analysis of what he calls disregard-that attacks, a class of prompt injection vulnerability where untrusted input in a large language model's context window tricks the model into ignoring its original instructions. The core argument is that the problem is architectural: sharing a context window with untrusted material is fundamentally unsafe, and layering more AI guardrails only triggers an arms race. For anyone building applications on top of language models, the takeaway is sobering.
