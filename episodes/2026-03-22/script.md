# Daily Briefing — Sunday, 22 March 2026

The Iran war has reached a dangerous new threshold. For the first time, missiles have struck near Israel's nuclear research centre, and Tehran has demonstrated it can reach targets thousands of kilometres away. Here is what matters today.

## World News

### Iran strikes towns near Israel's nuclear research centre at Dimona
source: BBC News
previously_covered: true
update_note: First targeting of Israel's nuclear site; Iranian missiles penetrate air defences near Dimona and Arad, injuring over 100

The conflict between Iran and Israel has entered its most dangerous phase yet. Iranian missiles struck the towns of Dimona and Arad in southern Israel on Saturday, injuring more than a hundred people. It was the first time Iranian missiles penetrated Israeli air defences in the area around the Negev nuclear research centre, and the first time the facility itself was targeted. Iran's Revolutionary Guard said the strikes were retaliation for what it described as an Israeli attack on the Natanz uranium enrichment complex. The International Atomic Energy Agency said it had received no reports of damage to the nuclear site or abnormal radiation levels, but the agency's chief called for maximum military restraint. The tit-for-tat targeting of nuclear facilities marks a stark escalation that puts the entire region on a new and far more precarious footing.

### Iran fires missiles at Diego Garcia, revealing range far beyond official claims
source: BBC News

Iran attempted to strike the joint British-American military base on Diego Garcia in the Indian Ocean on Saturday, firing two intermediate-range ballistic missiles at the atoll. Neither reached its target — one malfunctioned in flight and the other was intercepted. But the attempt itself is the story. Diego Garcia sits roughly four thousand kilometres from Iran, double the two-thousand-kilometre range Tehran has always stated publicly. The British Ministry of Defence condemned what it called a reckless attack. The implication that Iranian missiles could potentially reach southern Europe has sent a chill through defence ministries on the continent.

## Tech & Developer

### The three pillars of JavaScript bloat
source: Hacker News
hn_url: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47473718

A new blog post identifies three structural reasons why JavaScript bundles remain bloated despite years of effort. The first is legacy runtime support, where packages still protect against engine quirks that affect almost nobody. The second is atomic architecture, the trend of splitting functionality into dozens of tiny packages that creates supply chain risk without meaningful reuse. The third is outdated polyfills for features that have been natively supported for over a decade. The argument is that the cost of backward compatibility should fall on the few who need it, not on every project that pulls in a dependency.

### Armin Ronacher argues that some things just take time
source: Hacker News
hn_url: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47467537

Armin Ronacher, the creator of Flask and Jinja and now at Sentry, has published an essay pushing back against the culture of speed in software development. His core argument is that meaningful work — maintaining open-source projects, building trust with communities, understanding a problem deeply — requires sustained time that cannot be compressed by tooling or artificial intelligence. He points out that any efficiency gains from AI tools are immediately consumed by competitive pressure rather than creating genuine slack. The post has gathered over six hundred points on Hacker News and clearly struck a nerve.

### Ubuntu 26.04 ends forty-six years of silent sudo password entry
source: Hacker News
hn_url: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47464134

Ubuntu's next release will show asterisks when you type your password at a sudo prompt, ending a convention that dates back to the original Unix passwd command in nineteen eighty. The change sounds trivial, but the Hacker News discussion, with over three hundred comments, reveals just how strongly developers feel about terminal interaction patterns. The debate spans usability, security trade-offs, and whether showing password length is an acceptable information leak. After nearly half a century, the silent prompt is finally going away.

### European digital rights groups warn against turning child protection into internet access control
source: Hacker News
hn_url: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47470991

A coalition of European digital rights organisations has published a statement arguing that age verification systems, while framed as child protection, are fundamentally transforming the internet from open access to a permissioned architecture that requires identity verification. The concern is that this access control layer will expand well beyond age checks into broader surveillance. The authors argue that real child protection requires addressing manipulative algorithms and exploitative platform design, not building centralised identity systems that weaken privacy for everyone.
