Iran signals readiness to end the war, but the path is far from clear

Follow-up

Source: BBC News / Telex

Iran's president has spoken with the European Union Council president about ceasefire conditions, saying Tehran is ready to end the war but wants security guarantees before any deal. Donald Trump has weighed in from Washington, claiming the United States will resolve the conflict within two to three weeks. Meanwhile, the human cost continues to mount. Reports from the BBC say Iran is now using children in security roles, with witnesses describing an eleven-year-old killed in an air strike while manning a checkpoint in Tehran. The broader economic fallout is deepening too, with United States petrol prices crossing four dollars a gallon for the first time since the Ukraine conflict began, and fuel rationing and free bus schemes spreading across multiple countries.

Israel says it will hold a permanent buffer zone in southern Lebanon as peacekeepers are killed

Follow-up

Source: BBC News

Israel's defence minister has said the military will take permanent control of a large buffer zone in southern Lebanon and that houses in villages near the border will be demolished. Displaced Lebanese residents will not be allowed to return until security conditions are met, though what that means in practice remains undefined. In a grim development, two Indonesian United Nations peacekeepers were killed by a roadside explosion, less than a day after a third Indonesian peacekeeper died in a separate incident.

Source: BBC News

The Tisza Party's lead widens as Hungary's election enters its final days

Follow-up

Source: Telex

With Hungary's April election now imminent, the latest polls show Tisza pulling further ahead of Fidesz, leading 56 to 37 among committed voters. Source: Telex

Tech & Developer

Axios compromised on NPM in a major supply chain attack

Then & Now
Litellm packages on PyPI were confirmed compromised on 25 March, making this the second major package registry supply chain attack in a week.

Source: Hacker News

Malicious versions of Axios, one of the most widely used JavaScript HTTP libraries, have been published to NPM. The compromised packages drop a remote access trojan, and the story has become the most discussed item on Hacker News this week with nearly two thousand points. This comes barely a week after the litellm Python library was found compromised on PyPI. Two major package registries hit by supply chain attacks in the space of seven days is a worrying pattern, and developers relying on either library should audit their dependency trees immediately.

Claude Code's source code leaked through an NPM source map

Source: Hacker News

Anthropic's Claude Code command-line tool has had its source code exposed after a source map file was left in the NPM package registry. The leak has drawn enormous attention on Hacker News, with researchers picking through the code and finding what they describe as fake tools, frustration detection regexes, and an undercover mode. Anthropic has acknowledged the leak was caused by a developer packaging error rather than a security breach.
